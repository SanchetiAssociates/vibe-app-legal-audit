# United States law map for the audit (position as at 2 Oct 2026)

Confidence key: **V** = read from a fetched source this session. **K** = established statute known to the author but not re-fetched. Verify every **K** line before quoting it to a client. Penalty figures are statutory maxima and not typical outcomes.

## 1. Children (Check 1)

| Item | Position | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| Statute | COPPA, 15 U.S.C. 6501-6506. Rule at 16 CFR Part 312 | K |
| Civil penalty | Up to $53,088 per violation. FTC made no 2026 inflation adjustment (Federal Register, 15 Sep 2026) | V |
| 2025 Rule amendments | Published 22 Apr 2025. Effective 23 Jun 2025. Compliance deadline 22 Apr 2026 | V |
| What changed | Written data retention policy. Written security program. Wider definition of personal information (biometrics and government identifiers). More detail in direct notices to parents on third-party sharing. New "text plus" consent method. Mixed-audience site provisions. Safe harbor programs must disclose membership | V |
| Scope | Child-directed services and services with actual knowledge of under-13 users | K |
| States | California (and other states) add duties for minors under 16 on sale or sharing of data. Several states have age-appropriate design and app-store age verification laws that are in litigation. Check current status | K |

## 2. Privacy statutes (Checks 2, 3 and 7)

| Item | Position | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| CCPA and CPRA | Administrative fines of $2,500 per violation. $7,500 per intentional violation or violation involving minors under 16. Adjusted for inflation (about $2,663 and $7,988 in 2026 per one tracker) | V |
| Recent CCPA actions | General Motors $12.75M (May 2026). Disney $2.75M (Feb 2026). Tractor Supply $1.35M (Sep 2025). Healthline $1.55M. Honda $632,500. DoorDash $375,000 | V |
| Opt-out signals | CCPA treats Global Privacy Control as a valid opt-out of sale or sharing | K |
| Other states | Connecticut, Texas, Massachusetts and others enforce their own privacy laws. Check each state served | V |

## 3. Wiretap and tracking litigation (Check 3)

| Statute | Damages | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| California Penal Code 631 and 637.2 | $5,000 per violation or three times actual damages. No proof of harm needed | V |
| California Penal Code 638.51 (pen register theory) | $2,500 per violation. Courts split | V |
| Federal Wiretap Act (ECPA) | $10,000 or actual damages, whichever is greater. Crime-tort exception is unsettled | V |
| Florida Security of Communications Act | Up to $1,000 per violation per the source table. All-party consent | V |
| Pennsylvania WESCA | Actual damages plus punitive damages up to $100 a day or $1,000 and attorney fees. All-party consent | V |
| Others | Illinois, Maryland, Washington and Massachusetts are being tested. Massachusetts high court narrowed website wiretap theories in 2024 | V |

Defence trend: a California federal court held that data which becomes intelligible only after reassembly is not intercepted in transit. Ninth Circuit (Aug 2025) accepted limited standing defences. Reform bills stalled as of late 2025. Litigation is now multistate. Treat tracking tools as regulated data infrastructure.

## 4. Email and messaging (Check 4)

| Item | Position | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| CAN-SPAM Act | 15 U.S.C. 7701 et seq. and 16 CFR Part 316. Up to $53,088 per email. Requirements: accurate header and subject, identify the message as an ad, valid physical postal address, clear opt-out, honour opt-out within 10 business days, no fee for opting out, no sale of opted-out addresses | V |
| Enforcement | Verkada paid $2.95M (Aug 2024), the largest CAN-SPAM penalty then obtained | V |
| Text messages | Telephone Consumer Protection Act (47 U.S.C. 227) requires prior express consent for marketing texts and has statutory damages per message | K |

## 5. Subscriptions (Check 5)

| Item | Position | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| ROSCA | 15 U.S.C. 8401-8405. Disclose all material terms before taking billing information. Get express informed consent. Provide a simple way to stop recurring charges | V |
| FTC Negative Option ("click to cancel") Rule | Vacated in full by the Eighth Circuit in July 2025. FTC published an ANPRM on 11 Mar 2026 with comments closing 13 Apr 2026. Enforcement under ROSCA and FTC Act s.5 continues (settlements of $7.5M and $60M reported) | V |
| California ARL | Bus. & Prof. Code 17600-17606. AB 2863 applies to contracts entered into, amended or extended on or after 1 Jul 2025: express affirmative consent to renewal terms. Disclosure of price and frequency and cancellation method. Click-to-cancel button online. Same-medium cancellation. Annual reminders. Notice of price changes | V |
| Gift rule | Bus. & Prof. Code 17603: goods sent without compliant consent are an unconditional gift to the consumer | V |
| Other states | New York, Illinois, Colorado, Virginia and others have their own auto-renewal laws. Check each state served | K |

## 6. User uploads (Check 6)

| Item | Position | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| Safe harbor | 17 U.S.C. 512(c). Needs a designated agent listed with the Copyright Office, a notice-and-takedown process, no actual knowledge and a repeat-infringer policy (512(i)) | V and K |
| Agent fee | $6 per designation, amendment or resubmission. Renewal every 3 years | V |
| Statutory damages | $750 to $30,000 per work. Up to $150,000 if willful (17 U.S.C. 504(c)) | V |
| Registration limit | Statutory damages generally need timely registration of the work (17 U.S.C. 411 and 412) | K |
